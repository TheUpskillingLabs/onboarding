Welcome to The Upskilling Labs volunteer community!

We\'re grateful you\'re here. Volunteers are not just support staff. At
The Upskilling Labs, everything we do is made possible by volunteers.
From mentoring project teams to managing events, building our digital
infrastructure to telling our community\'s story, volunteers are the
engine that keeps the Labs running and growing.

The Labs is an open, project-based learning community where people build
real things with real people. Every quarter, we run a Build Cycle themed
around an industry. Participants form pods around real problems, develop
AI-enabled solutions together, and showcase what they\'ve built. No
credentials required. Just show up ready to learn alongside others. As a
volunteer, you\'re not just supporting that mission. You\'re part of it.

YOUR ROLE

Volunteers support the Labs across seven teams. Each team has its own
rhythm, responsibilities, and time commitment. You\'ll be matched based
on your skills and interests. And within your team, you\'ll have clear
responsibilities and a point of contact on the Labs organizing team.

We do not view any volunteer role as static. As the Labs grows, roles
evolve. Bring your ideas, flag what\'s not working, and help us build
something better.

PARTICIPANTS (UPSKILLERS), VOLUNTEER TEAMS & ROLES

👷 **Upskillers**

An Upskiller is a participant in The Upskilling Labs community ---
someone who shows up to learn, build, and contribute without waiting for
credentials or permission.

Upskillers who join build cycles work in small research communities
called Pods, tackle real problems using AI-enabled approaches, and form
small project teams to build solutions. The role is peer-led and
project-based: Upskillers aren\'t passive students --- they\'re active
builders navigating a period of massive technological change alongside
others in the community. The term signals belonging and agency; it\'s
the organization\'s way of saying you\'re already someone who does this
work.

🧠 **Mentors**

We don\'t view mentorship as a static title or a social hierarchy ---
it\'s an active practice. Mentors are expert peers: colleagues and
community members who possess specialized knowledge and offer it to
those navigating similar paths. That expertise comes in many forms:
industry knowledge relevant to the current build cycle, technical depth
(especially AI), and soft skills like design thinking, storytelling, and
user research.

Mentors support the build cycles in two ways:

-   Workshop Facilitators design and deliver hands-on sessions across
    > three tracks: soft skills (design thinking, storytelling, customer
    > discovery), technology (prompt engineering, vibe coding, AI
    > tools), and sector knowledge (healthcare AI, HIPAA, civic
    > engagement, climate and energy, and other industry topics).
    > Sessions can be held in-person or virtually.

-   Open Office Hours --- scheduled sessions (virtual or in-person) ---
    > when a team hits a technical or tactical wall, the organizing team
    > issues a call to mentors with relevant expertise. The mentor
    > engages for a targeted 20-to-30-minute session to help the team
    > think through the problem rather than solving it for them.

Time commitment:

-   Lead workshops, *4 hours/quarter*

-   Host Office Hours, *1 hour/week*

🏛️ **Governance & Grant Writing**

The Governance Team provides strategic oversight and ensures the
organization stays true to its mission, makes sound decisions, and has
the resources needed to grow. Grant Writing volunteers research funding
opportunities and draft grant applications to support the Labs\'
programming, tools, and community growth.

Time commitment: *4--10 hours/week (varies by grant cycle)*

💻 **Research & Development (Operating System)**

The R&D Team builds and maintains the technical infrastructure that
powers the Labs --- from our operating system to the tools that enable
seamless community engagement. Web & Tools Development volunteers
support the development and maintenance of our digital ecosystem,
including the community tools, and any technology that supports program
delivery.

Time commitment: *4--10 hours/week*

⚙️ **Programming & Events**

The Programming Team organizes a variety of events including
roundtables, workshops, and meetups. Two key roles:

-   Day-of Volunteers --- support event logistics on the ground,
    > handling registration, room setup, and attendee coordination to
    > ensure everything runs smoothly.

-   Event Management Volunteers --- help with venue coordination,
    > scheduling, speaker logistics, and run-of-show planning in the
    > weeks leading up to each event.

Time commitment:

-   4-6 hours/week (day of support)

-   72 hours/quarter (event management)

📣 **Communications and Marketing**

The Comms Team is the voice and visual identity of The Upskilling Labs.
We\'re not just a marketing function --- we handle branding, PR,
philanthropic communications, and storytelling across all channels. We
also support programming and engagement teams with internal
communications, keeping participants informed and inspired every step of
the way. Three focused roles:

-   Content Writing --- craft compelling written content that brings the
    > Labs\' mission and community stories to life. This includes weekly
    > newsletters (Mailchimp), blog posts and event recaps, workshop and
    > meetup announcements, and brochures and partner communications.

-   Design --- create visual assets that reinforce our brand identity
    > across every touchpoint: event flyers, slide deck templates,
    > branded social media graphics, and visual identity for campaigns.

-   Social Media --- manage the Labs\' social presence, schedule and
    > publish LinkedIn posts, engage with followers, amplify event
    > announcements, highlight member spotlights, and track engagement
    > insights.

-   Digital fundraising - use online platforms, social media, email, and
    > websites to collect donations, engage donors, and run campaigns.

Time commitment: *4-10 hours/week per role*

🤝 **Participant Success (Pod Moderators)**

The Participant Success Team supports Upskillers throughout their
journey, ensuring people are engaged, supported, and making progress
toward their goals. Pod Moderators (Poderators) provide structure and
support to teams during the build cycle. Their role is to maintain
clarity of purpose, healthy working dynamics, and steady progress within
a Pod --- without directing the work, owning outcomes, or solving
problems for participants.

Poderators encourage Upskillers to complete weekly check-in forms,
communicate with other volunteers about participant needs and feedback,
and serve as an early warning system when a team is struggling.

Time commitment: *4-10 hours/week via Slack and virtual meetings.
Minimum of 4 volunteers needed per cycle.*

THE DOS

Do show up consistently.

Reliability is the most important thing you can offer. If you\'ve
committed to a task, a shift, or a deadline, follow through. The rest of
the team and the participants we serve are counting on you.

Do communicate early when something comes up.

Life happens. If you\'re going to miss a deadline, can\'t make an event,
or need to step back temporarily, say so as soon as you know. A heads-up
with time to adjust is always better than silence. Reach out in Slack or
contact your team lead directly.

Do ask questions when you get stuck.

If you\'re unsure about a task, a deadline, or how something should be
done --- ask. Don\'t spend an hour guessing when a two-minute Slack
message would get you unstuck. There are no dumb questions here, only
missed opportunities to learn faster.

Do stay in your lane --- and own it fully.

Each role has a defined scope for good reason. Know what you own, do it
well, and resist the urge to take on more than you can handle. A
volunteer who owns one lane and delivers consistently is more valuable
than one who stretches across five and drops the ball.

Do stay on brand.

The Labs has a voice and a visual identity. When you\'re writing,
posting, designing, or speaking on behalf of the organization, represent
us consistently. When in doubt, refer to the brand guidelines or ask
before publishing.

Do treat participants, staff, and fellow volunteers with respect.

The Labs is built on the belief that everyone has something to learn and
something to contribute. Create an environment where people feel safe to
show up, ask questions, and try new things. Be kind, be patient, and be
encouraging.

Do celebrate the work.

Notice when something goes well --- a workshop that lands, a post that
resonates, a participant who finally figures something out. Say so.
Positive feedback is fuel for a volunteer community.

THE DON\'TS

Don\'t ghost team members.

Disappearing without notice is the most disruptive thing a volunteer can
do. If tasks go unfinished and no one knows why, it creates extra work
for everyone else and puts participants in a tough spot. If you\'re
struggling, overloaded, or thinking about stepping back --- tell us. We
would rather adjust than lose you.

Don\'t post or publish without approval.

All external-facing content (social media posts, newsletters, event
announcements, press outreach) needs to go through the review process
before it goes live. This protects the organization and ensures our
communications are consistent and accurate. When in doubt, draft and
ask.

Don\'t make decisions on behalf of the organization.

Volunteers support and execute. They don\'t set strategy, make
commitments to external partners, or speak officially on behalf of the
Labs unless explicitly authorized. If someone asks you a question
you\'re not sure about, say \"let me connect you with our team\" and
loop in your lead.

Don\'t do the work for participants.

This applies especially to Mentors and Pod Moderators. No matter how
much faster it would be, resist solving problems directly for
participants. Ask guiding questions, point to resources, help them
think, but let them do the work. That\'s how learning happens.

Don\'t work around the system.

All volunteer coordination happens through Slack and the Labs\' shared
tools. Don\'t set up separate channels, side conversations, or external
workflows without talking to the team first. Keeping things visible and
centralized is how we stay organized and make sure nothing falls through
the cracks.

Don\'t lose patience.

Everyone at the Labs is at a different point in their journey. What
feels obvious to you may be brand new to someone else. Lead with
patience and meet people where they are. What\'s second nature to you
now was once new to you too.

TOOLS AND COMMUNICATION

The Labs runs on a small, intentional tech stack. Here\'s what you\'ll
use and how:

-   Slack: Primary communication platform. Check it regularly, respond
    > within 24 hours on weekdays, and keep your status updated.

-   Google Workspace: Docs, Sheets, and Drive for shared documents,
    > editorial calendars, and volunteer resources. Use the shared
    > folders, don\'t create parallel systems.

-   Luma: Events and registration management. Volunteers supporting
    > events will be given access to relevant event pages.

-   Mailchimp: Used by the Comms team for newsletters and email
    > communications.

-   Email: Used for formal communications and onboarding. For day-to-day
    > coordination, Slack is preferred.

YOUR COMMITMENT

Volunteer roles at the Labs are organized around our quarterly Build
Cycles. At the start of each cycle, you\'ll confirm your availability
and the team you\'re supporting. At the end of each cycle, you\'ll have
the opportunity to renew, shift roles, or step back --- no guilt, no
awkward conversations.

Time commitments vary by role and are listed above. Be honest about your
capacity when you sign up. Overcommitting and underdelivering is harder
to recover from than setting realistic expectations upfront.

If your availability changes during a cycle, tell your team lead as
early as possible. We\'d rather adjust expectations mid-cycle than have
work fall through the cracks.

WHEN IN DOUBT

Ask yourself: \"Am I making the Labs easier to run, or harder?\" That\'s
the clearest signal for whether you\'re on the right track.

If you\'re unsure whether to post something, commit to something, or
take action on something --- pause and ask. The Labs team would always
rather answer a question than clean up a misunderstanding.

And if something isn\'t working such as a process, a tool, or a role
definition, say so. We\'re a learning community, and that means the way
we run things should keep getting better too.

Thank you for being part of the Labs.

Your time, skills, and presence make the work possible. If you have
questions about these guidelines

or need support in your volunteer role, reach out to the Labs team
anytime.
